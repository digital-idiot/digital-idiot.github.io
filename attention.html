1 of 6 · Correlation

A dot product measures how well two vectors line up

Multiply matching elements, then add. Centre first and you get the covariance; also scale each vector to unit length and you get the correlation.

\[ a\cdot b=\sum_i a_i b_i=\lVert a\rVert\,\lVert b\rVert\cos\theta \] \[ \operatorname{cov}(a,b)=\tfrac{1}{n-1}\sum_i (a_i-\bar a)(b_i-\bar b) \] \[ r=\frac{\operatorname{cov}(a,b)}{\sigma_a\sigma_b}=\sum_i \hat a_i\,\hat b_i \] \[ \hat a=\frac{a-\bar a}{\lVert a-\bar a\rVert},\qquad \hat b=\frac{b-\bar b}{\lVert b-\bar b\rVert} \]

Try Opposite with the plain dot product, then with correlation. Next: compare a signal with a shifted copy of itself.

2 of 6 · Autocorrelation

Correlate a signal with its own past

Shift the signal by k steps and correlate it with the original. Repeat for every k and you get the autocorrelation function.

\[ \rho(k)=\frac{\sum_t (x_t-\bar x)(x_{t+k}-\bar x)}{\sum_t (x_t-\bar x)^2} \]

The peak at k = 12 is the signal's period: the past that is most similar. Next: compare two different sets of vectors, all pairs at once.

4 of 6 · Scale and softmax

Divide by \(\sqrt{d_k}\) before the softmax

Dot products of random vectors grow with width. Unscaled, softmax snaps to one key and its gradient vanishes.

\[ \operatorname{Var}(q\cdot k)=d_k \] \[ a_j=\operatorname{softmax}_j\!\Big(\frac{q\cdot k_j}{\sqrt{d_k}}\Big)=\frac{e^{s_j}}{\sum_l e^{s_l}} \]

6 of 6 · Self‑attention

Self‑attention is a learned, content‑based autocorrelation

Each hour is embedded by its standardised value and slope. Averaging the scores along each diagonal gives the autocorrelation of that embedding.

\[ \text{Attention}(Q,K,V)=\operatorname{softmax}\!\Big(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\Big)V \] \[ \frac{1}{T-k}\sum_t x_t\cdot x_{t+k}\;\propto\;\rho_x(k) \]
